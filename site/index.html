The change was one line, and it was reasonable

Public health coverage is an operational field. A benefits team needs it. It carries no health detail, it is not a protected characteristic, and no honest governance policy would tag it as one.

Below 65 it is also close to a disability field without being one. Medicare there requires SSDI, end stage renal disease or ALS. The Medicaid pathways include the Supplemental Security Income route. So the column arrives looking like admin and lands like a diagnosis.

The model was never told about disability. It was never given the column. It got better at knowing anyway.

How it knows

The column is untagged on the feature table, because nobody tags a column they never thought about. Ariadne follows it backwards through the warehouse until it reaches something that is tagged, and then keeps going until it reaches the raw census file.

  1. 0workforce_features.public_coverage_flaguntagged, feeds a model in Production
  2. 1dim_person.public_coverage_flagpersonal_data
  3. 2stg_person.public_coverage_flagstaging
  4. 3raw_person.pubcovthe real census variable name

Every edge here was produced by a DataHub connector reading dbt, Postgres and MLflow. None of it is hand written. That matters, because a lineage graph you drew yourself proves nothing about a warehouse you did not.

Why nothing else caught it

Accuracy monitors

They were not asleep. They were reporting an improvement. Accuracy rose +0.0355, which any team would ship. On the lending model the same class of change moved accuracy by 0.0001, and no threshold tight enough to catch that survives contact with ordinary retraining noise.

Tag and policy scanners

They were correct and blind at once. The coverage flag is genuinely not a protected characteristic, so a scanner that reads labels finds nothing, and it is right not to. A tool that fired here on the label alone would be wrong for the right reason.

Fairness dashboards

They compare outcomes across groups you already collected and named. This is a change in what the model can infer, not in what it was told. Sex is absent from the lending model and tagged nowhere, and is still recoverable from its features at 0.813.

One warehouse, one engine, three legal regimes

The rules are declared, not compiled in. A regime file names attributes and never names columns, which is what lets it move between warehouses. Point the same checks at a hiring model instead of a lending model and the answers change because the law changes.

Age is conditional under ECOA because Regulation B carves out an empirically derived credit scoring system. The ADEA has no such carve out, so the same column is flatly protected for a worker over forty. Same column, same warehouse, different answer.

Two routes, one finding

route one

Statistical, no labels

The delta watch measured how much of disability the model could rebuild from the features it does hold, before and after. It moved 12 times the noise. It needed nobody to have labelled anything.

route two

By tag, under the right law

The EU AI Act asks for examination of bias affecting fundamental rights rather than listing forbidden columns, so it watches personal data too. Under that regime the tag check finds the same column, one hop back.

Neither layer is a backup for the other. Labels catch a column somebody named. The measurement catches information nobody named. Getting the same answer twice by different means is the closest thing to corroboration this problem allows.

The column was not a guess

The first attempt guessed. A commute feasibility field, vehicles per household, seemed like a plausible stand in for disability. It moved the number by +0.0095 and stayed silent, correctly.

So the candidates came from the disability literature instead, and every one was tested against the actual warehouse rather than trusted. Nineteen went in. Four cleared the bar.

  • PUBCOVpublic health coverage+0.0510
  • HINS4Medicaid+0.0342
  • SSIPsupplemental security income+0.0250
  • HINS3Medicare+0.0229
  • POVPIPincome to poverty ratio+0.0177
  • VEHvehicles available, the guess+0.0095
  • GRPIPrent as a share of income+0.0053

Rent burden is the useful failure. The association between housing cost burden and disability is well documented in the census literature, and it does not survive the screen here. A published finding is a hypothesis about your warehouse, never a fact about it. That is the whole reason the testing half exists.

It files itself

A finding that lives in a terminal is a finding nobody acts on. Ariadne raises the result as a DataHub incident on the feature table, carrying the column, the statute, the citation and the numbers, so the person who opens the table has what they need.

Filed on the table rather than the model, because DataHub does not accept an incident on an ML model. The table is the better home anyway: it is where the column entered and where a fix goes.

What it does not do

It does not decide legality. Correlation with a protected attribute is not prohibited and cannot be, because income and education correlate with race and a model stripped of everything correlated with race predicts nothing. The law asks about harm, business necessity and less harmful alternatives. Those are human calls.

It is not fully label free. Knowing that disability_code means disability came from a declared policy file. Lineage then carries that one fact everywhere it needs to go, so you only have to be right once, but once is not zero.

Public coverage is not purely disability. In California after Medicaid expansion it also tracks low income. Medicare below 65 is the cleaner channel. The number is real and the reading of it needs care.

The absolute level is hard to argue about. Race sits at 0.73 from an ordinary lending model, which could be plain socioeconomic correlation. The change is the trustworthy signal, which is why the alarm watches movement, not level.
